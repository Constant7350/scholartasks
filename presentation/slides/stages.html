Younger students

Only what fits their grade, with a dream to aim at

  • Three stages: Explore (6–8), Build (9–10), Apply (11–12)
  • Menu, Home, scholarships and colleges fit the stage; in grades 6–8 colleges and scholarships are for parents, under Look ahead
  • Dream board: two or three dreams in their own words, and someone they look up to
  • The AI counselor and Homework Helper connect this year’s classes to those dreams
  • Nothing is removed: parents can show every page any time, and the full app opens in grade 11

ScholarTasks · scholartasks.com

Younger students need a different app than seniors, and now they get one, inside the same app. A sixth, seventh or eighth grader sees the Explore pages: the career quiz, grades, study skills, the Homework Helper and careers to try. Ninth and tenth graders add the course plan, activities, the PSAT and a first look at colleges. Juniors and seniors see everything. In grades six to eight, colleges and scholarships are for parents only, behind one Look ahead button, so a seventh grader's own app stays simple. From ninth grade, scholarships match the student's grade, and until eleventh grade the college page is for exploring, not deadlines. On Home, a younger student writes two or three dreams in their own words, and someone they look up to. The AI counselor and the Homework Helper use that to show how this year's math class or that robotics club leads there. Nothing is taken away: a parent can switch on every page any time, and the full app opens by itself in grade eleven.
